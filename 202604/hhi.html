Introduction

Imagine you wake up with a high fever. In many countries, you might worry about how much a doctor’s visit will cost. In Taiwan, you can walk into a nearby clinic, show your health insurance card, and see a doctor on the same day for a small fee. This convenience comes from the National Health Insurance, or NHI, which many experts see as a model for the world.

What Is the NHI?

Taiwan started the NHI on March 1, 1995. Before that, only some groups of people, such as workers and government employees, had insurance. Today, the NHI is a single-payer system. This means one government agency, the National Health Insurance Administration, collects the money and pays hospitals and clinics. About 99 percent of the people in Taiwan are covered, including many foreign residents.

How It Works

Everyone pays a monthly premium based on their income. The cost is shared by households, employers, and the government. When patients see a doctor, they pay only a small co-payment. They can choose any hospital or clinic in the system, and they do not need a referral from another doctor.

The NHI gives broad coverage. It pays for check-ups, surgery, prescriptions, dental care, and traditional Chinese medicine. It even pays for very expensive treatment for cancer and rare diseases. In this way, everyone has access to good care at an affordable price.

A World-Class System

In 2026, the website Numbeo ranked Taiwan first in the world in its Health Care Index, with a score of about 87 out of 100. Taiwan has been at or near the top for several years. The index is based on surveys about people’s own experiences, such as quality, waiting time, cost, and convenience. It is not a medical test of hospitals, but it shows that people in Taiwan are satisfied with their care.

The system is also efficient. Taiwan spends only about US$2,400 per person each year on healthcare, while the United States spends about US$13,500. Even so, Taiwan receives a much higher score.

Challenges Ahead

No system is perfect. As Taiwan’s population grows older and new treatments become more expensive, the yearly budget keeps rising. It is now more than one trillion New Taiwan dollars. In the past, spending has often grown faster than income, so the government must manage the money carefully. Using medical services wisely is also everyone’s responsibility.

Conclusion

Taiwan’s NHI shows that it is possible to give almost everyone access to good medical care at a reasonable cost. Other countries can learn from this experience. At the same time, it reminds us that a good system needs constant care and smart planning.
