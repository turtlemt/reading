Introduction

Have you ever had a shot at school or at a clinic? Many of these shots are vaccines. A vaccine does not cure a disease. Instead, it helps your body get ready before the disease arrives. This idea is called prevention, and it has saved millions of lives. The story of the first vaccine began more than 200 years ago with a country doctor in England.

How a Vaccine Works

Your immune system protects you from germs. When a new germ enters your body, the immune system learns to fight it by making antibodies. This takes time, so a dangerous germ can make you very sick before your body is ready. A vaccine shows the immune system a weak or harmless piece of the germ. The body practices fighting it and remembers how. Later, if the real germ appears, the body can respond quickly. When many people are vaccinated, the disease also has a hard time spreading to those who cannot be vaccinated.

A Deadly Disease

In the 1700s, smallpox was one of the most feared diseases in the world. It caused high fevers and painful sores, and many patients died. Those who survived often had scars for life. Doctors had no real cure.

Edward Jenner’s Discovery

Edward Jenner was a doctor in the English countryside. He noticed that milkmaids who had caught cowpox, a mild disease from cows, seldom caught smallpox. He believed that cowpox might protect people from the deadly disease.

In May 1796, Jenner tested his idea. He took fluid from a cowpox sore on a milkmaid named Sarah Nelmes and put it into small cuts on the arm of James Phipps, an eight-year-old boy. The boy had a mild illness and quickly got better. Weeks later, Jenner exposed the boy to smallpox. The boy did not get sick. Jenner’s idea worked. He later published his results, and the method spread across Europe and the world.

A Disease Defeated

The word “vaccine” comes from the Latin word vacca, which means cow. Over time, countries ran large vaccination programs. In 1980, the World Health Organization announced that smallpox had been wiped out. It remains the only human disease that has been completely eradicated.

Conclusion

Jenner’s experiment would not be allowed today, because modern science has strict rules to protect patients. However, his careful observation changed medicine forever. Today, vaccines protect us from many serious diseases. The next time you get a shot, remember that it is the result of a long journey that began with a curious doctor and a milkmaid.
