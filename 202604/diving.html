Introduction

Imagine floating quietly under the sea, surrounded by colorful fish and beautiful coral. For many people, this is not a dream. It is a favorite hobby. Diving lets us visit a world that is completely different from the land. Taiwan is an island surrounded by ocean, so it has many great places to try this exciting activity.

Three Ways to Dive

There are three main kinds of recreational diving. The easiest is snorkeling. You float on the surface with a mask, a snorkel, and fins, and look down at the sea life. You do not need much training, but you should be able to swim.

The second kind is freediving. Freedivers hold their breath and dive without a tank. They train their bodies to stay calm and use less oxygen, so they can go deeper and stay under longer.

The third kind is scuba diving, which is the most popular way to explore deeper water. “Scuba” stands for Self-Contained Underwater Breathing Apparatus. Divers carry a tank of air on their backs and breathe through a regulator, so they can stay underwater for about 40 to 60 minutes.

How to Learn

Most beginners start with a short trial lesson. An instructor teaches you the basics in a pool or in calm, shallow water. If you enjoy it, you can take an Open Water Diver course. Groups such as PADI and SSI offer this course all over the world, and students aged ten and older can join a junior course.

The course has three parts. First, you study theory, such as water pressure, equipment, and safety rules. Next, you practice basic skills in a pool. Finally, you make real dives in the sea. After you pass, you receive a certification card. It allows you to dive with a buddy down to 18 meters.

Safety First

Diving is safe when you follow the rules. Learn how to equalize the pressure in your ears as you go down. Never hold your breath when you use scuba equipment. Always ascend slowly, and never dive alone. Check the weather and your equipment before every dive.

Conclusion

Diving is more than a sport. It is a way to learn about the ocean and to understand why we should protect it. With good training and a careful attitude, almost anyone can enjoy it. Maybe your own underwater adventure is only one lesson away.
