Introduction

Every day, people burn coal, oil, and gas to run cars, factories, and power plants. This puts a lot of carbon dioxide into the air. Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas, which means it traps heat around the Earth and leads to global warming. Fortunately, nature has a helper that works quietly all day long: trees. Planting trees is one of the simplest ways to protect our planet.

How Trees Make Food

A tree is like a small factory powered by sunlight. This process is called photosynthesis. The leaves contain chlorophyll, a green substance that captures light energy. The tree absorbs carbon dioxide from the air through tiny holes in its leaves, and it takes in water through its roots. Using the energy from sunlight, the tree changes them into glucose and oxygen. Glucose is a sugar that feeds the tree, and the oxygen is released into the air for us to breathe.

Locking Away Carbon

The glucose does not disappear. The tree uses it to build new leaves, branches, roots, and wood. In this way, carbon from the air becomes part of the tree’s body. Scientists call this carbon fixation. A large tree can absorb about 20 kilograms of carbon dioxide in a year, and it can keep that carbon locked inside for decades or even centuries. A healthy forest is like a giant carbon bank.

More Benefits of Trees

Trees do much more than clean the air. Their shade can make a hot city street feel noticeably cooler. Their roots hold the soil together, which reduces erosion and helps prevent landslides during heavy rain. Forests are also the habitat of countless birds, insects, and mammals. Even a small park can make people feel calmer and happier.

Plant the Right Tree

Planting trees helps the most when we do it carefully. Native species usually grow best because they fit the local climate and support local wildlife. Young trees absorb only a small amount of carbon at first, so we must also protect old forests and take care of new trees for many years. Planting a tree and then forgetting it is not enough.

Conclusion

A tree is an air cleaner, a cooling system, and a home for wildlife, all powered by the sun. Trees alone cannot solve climate change, but every tree helps. Perhaps your school or community will hold a tree-planting day soon. It could be your chance to make a real difference.
